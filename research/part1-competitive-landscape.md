# Part 1 - Market & Competitive Landscape

## Intro: the space

**Menopause, in a few words:** it's when a woman's periods stop for good, usually around 45-55. The hard part often starts earlier, in perimenopause, when hormones swing and the symptoms hit - hot flashes, broken sleep, brain fog, mood swings. Every woman goes through it, it lasts years, and for a long time nobody built for it.

**The vertical** is women's health, specifically the menopause and perimenopause space. **The category** is menopause wellness - skincare, body, intimate care and supplements, all hormone-free. Stripes is a life-stage brand: everything is tied to this one moment, so one customer can buy across all four types. Our hero product, The Inside Addition, is the daily supplement.

## Who it's for

Women roughly 42-62 in peri/menopause who want relief. She doesn't want hormones (or can't take them), feels ignored by doctors and by beauty brands that stopped talking to her, has tried things that didn't work, and wants this to feel normal, not scary.

## How I define competitors

Two yeses: same woman (peri/menopausal) and same need (ease her symptoms). If both, she's a competitor. Then one question sorts it: a menopause brand selling a product like ours is **direct**; a different answer, or a bigger brand catching her through one symptom, is **indirect**.
- **Direct:** Womaness, Kindra, Bonafide, Estroven, Wile, Pause Well-Aging, Valerie.
- **Indirect:** Happy Mammoth (hormones/gut), Midi/Alloy/Evernow (HRT), O Positiv (vaginal probiotics), Nutrafol (hair), Ritual (vitamins), Foria/Good Clean Love/Joylux (intimacy).

What the data shows:
- The biggest paid players are **indirect, non-menopause brands**, and each leans on a different channel: **display** for O Positiv (~59%, though a chunk looks like low-quality/programmatic traffic), **paid social** for Happy Mammoth (~20%, its standout channel), and **paid search** for the telehealth/HRT brands (Midi, Alloy) buying "hormone replacement" intent.
- The **menopause-native brands barely run paid.** The one real exception is Bonafide (~42% of its traffic, mostly search + display); the rest live on organic and email.
- Stripes isn't in the paid game yet (paid is a rounding error of ~11K total traffic) and isn't advertising the supplement.
