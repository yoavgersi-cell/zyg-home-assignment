# Part 1 - Market & Competitive Landscape

## What is menopause?

Menopause is the point where a woman's body stops having periods for good, and it usually lands somewhere between 45 and 55. But the part that actually turns her life upside down often starts years earlier, in perimenopause, when her hormones start swinging up and down. That's when the symptoms show up: hot flashes, night sweats, broken sleep, brain fog, and a mood that changes faster than she can catch it.

What makes this a real market and not a footnote is simple. Every woman goes through it, it can drag on for years, and for a long time almost nobody talked about it or built products for it. Stripes was built for the whole ride, perimenopause and menopause both. That's not an accident: Naomi Watts hit perimenopause in her mid-30s and got blindsided by it, and that's exactly why the brand exists.

## The vertical and the category

The vertical is women's health, and more specifically the menopause and perimenopause corner of it. The category is menopause wellness.

Stripes sells a few different product types inside that world - skincare, body care, intimate care, and supplements - and all of it is hormone-free. Our hero product, The Inside Addition, is the daily supplement.

The thing to understand is that Stripes isn't really a skincare brand or a supplement brand. It's a life-stage brand. Everything it makes is tied to one moment in a woman's life, which means a single customer can buy across all four product types. That's the whole model.

## Who it's for

She's a woman in perimenopause or menopause who just wants to feel better, and she's roughly early 40s to early 60s. A few things tend to be true about her:

- She doesn't want to take hormones, or she can't, so "hormone-free" genuinely matters to her.
- She feels ignored, by doctors who wave her off and by beauty brands that stop talking to women her age.
- She's probably tried something before that did nothing, so she comes in a little skeptical.
- And she wants this to feel normal, even a bit fun. Not clinical, not scary.

## How we decide who's a competitor

It's tempting to only count "menopause brands," but that's the mistake. In a paid feed she isn't browsing a tidy menopause shelf - she's getting served ads by anyone chasing the same problem she has. So the test I use is two simple yeses:

- **Same woman?** Is it aimed at a woman in peri or menopause?
- **Same need?** Is she buying it to feel better and ease her symptoms?

If both are yes, it's a competitor. Then one more question sorts it: is this a menopause brand selling a product like ours, something she'd buy instead of The Inside Addition? If yes, it's **direct**. If it's a different kind of answer - a prescription or HRT service, or a bigger brand that catches her through just one symptom - it's **indirect**. And those indirect players are usually the ones fighting hardest for her in the feed.

## A quick note on the data

Everything that follows is based on the last three months, April through June 2026. I pulled the traffic and channel numbers from Similarweb and Semrush, and the live ad counts from the Meta Ad Library. It's estimated data, so I treat it as direction, not gospel - but I looked at around 15 brands and the story didn't wobble.

## The two groups

**Direct** - menopause is the whole brand: Womaness, Kindra, Bonafide, Estroven, Wile, Pause Well-Aging, Valerie.

**Indirect** - a bigger or different brand that scoops her up along the way: Happy Mammoth (hormones and gut), Midi, Alloy and Evernow (HRT telehealth), O Positiv (vaginal probiotics), Nutrafol (hair), Ritual (vitamins), and the intimate-wellness set - Foria, Good Clean Love, Joylux.

There's also a long tail of smaller brands and retailers (Honey Girl Organics, marketplaces like Thirteen Lune) that don't really show up on paid, so I've left them as footnotes.

## Who's actually spending to win her

Here's where it gets interesting. Ranked by traffic and paid social (Stripes left out, since that's us):

| Brand | Type | Visits / mo | Paid social / mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| O Positiv | Indirect | ~5.8M | ~234K |
| Midi | Indirect | ~3.6M | ~421K |
| Happy Mammoth | Indirect | ~2.5M | ~632K |
| Alloy | Indirect | ~1.7M | ~119K |
| Bonafide | Direct | ~1.1M | ~46K |
| Estroven / Kindra / Womaness | Direct | ~40-250K | ~0.2-1.8K |

Two things jumped out. The brands pulling the most traffic aren't menopause brands at all - they buy their growth, and organic search is a rounding error for them. And the actual menopause brands barely run paid; apart from Bonafide, they live on SEO and email.

## Where we sit

Stripes is doing about 11K visits a month, almost none of it from paid social (~100 visits), and we're not even running paid behind the supplement yet. So the honest read is that we're not late to a fight we're losing - we just haven't shown up to it. That's the opportunity, not the problem.

## The gap I'd bet on

Paid social isn't empty. But look at what she's actually offered there: a hormone pill she doesn't quite trust (Happy Mammoth), "go get on HRT" (Midi and Alloy), vaginal probiotics (O Positiv), a pelvic device (Joylux), a faceless clinical supplement (Bonafide). Not one of them is a credible, hormone-free daily with a face she actually trusts.

That's the empty seat. And it's the exact one Stripes is built to sit in: Naomi Watts, hormone-free, premium, clearly made for this woman.

## So what

We can't outspend a brand doing 2.5M visits a month, and we shouldn't try. The play is to own the lane none of them are standing in, and to plant it on paid social - where attention is cheapest, and where, honestly, no real menopause brand is even showing up.
