# Part 1 — Market & Competitive Landscape

**Data basis:** Similarweb + Semrush (traffic and channel mix) and Meta Ad Library (active ads). Window: April–June 2026 (last 3 months). ~15 brands reviewed. All figures are estimates and read as directional.

## 1. Category & vertical
- **Vertical:** women's health, specifically the menopause and perimenopause space.
- **Category:** menopause wellness (skincare, body, intimate care, supplements — all hormone-free).
- **Hero product's lane:** daily symptom-support supplement — the most competitive, most ad-driven corner.
- **Audience:** women roughly 42–62, symptomatic, HRT-hesitant or HRT-excluded, often burned by a generic supplement that did nothing.

## 2. How competitors are defined
A competitor = **same woman** (peri/menopausal) + **same need** (symptom relief). Sorted by one question: *is menopause the brand's whole identity?*
- **Direct** (menopause is the brand): Womaness, Kindra, Bonafide, Estroven, Wile, Pause Well-Aging, Valerie.
- **Indirect** (broader brand that captures her, usually via paid): Happy Mammoth (hormones/gut), Midi / Alloy / Evernow (HRT telehealth), O Positiv (vaginal probiotics), Nutrafol (hair), Ritual (vitamins), Foria / Good Clean Love / Joylux (intimate wellness).
- **Long tail (not competing on paid):** Honey Girl Organics; retailers like Thirteen Lune.

| O Positiv | Indirect | ~5.8M | ~234K |

*(Stripes for context, not a competitor: ~11K visits/mo, ~0.1K paid social.)*

## 4. Key findings
1. **The category's paid leaders are not menopause brands.** The top of the table is entirely indirect (hormones/gut, HRT, vaginal probiotics). They buy their growth; organic search is 3–6% of traffic.
2. **Menopause-native brands barely run paid.** Except Bonafide, they rely on SEO + email; paid social is a few hundred to ~2K visits/mo.
3. **Paid social is contested only by "other" answers:** HRT (Midi/Alloy), a hype supplement (Happy Mammoth), vaginal probiotics (O Positiv), a pelvic device (Joylux), a faceless clinical supplement (Bonafide).

## 5. The whitespace
No brand owns **trusted face (Naomi Watts) + hormone-free + premium + built for her** on paid social. That position is unoccupied.

## 6. Where Stripes stands
~11K visits/mo, ~100 paid-social visits, no paid behind the supplement yet. Stripes has not entered the paid race — so the launch is greenfield, not a catch-up.

## 7. Implication for the launch
Do not compete on spend (a 2.5M-visit machine is unbeatable on budget). Compete on **angle + credibility**, and lead on **paid social**: cheapest attention, and empty of credible menopause-native competitors.

# Part 2 — Hero Product Launch

## 2.1 Creative angle
**Angle: "The daily that meets menopause where it actually hits."**
Lead line: *"You were told to just push through the 2 a.m. wake-ups, the fog, the mood swings. From Naomi Watts — one hormone-free daily, made to give you your nights, your focus and your calm back."*

- **Why this angle:** it is symptom-specific (sleep → mood → focus), which stops the scroll where category education can't. It leads with the three things no competitor on paid social has together: **a face she trusts (Naomi Watts), hormone-free, and real science.** And its tone is empowered, not fear-based.
- **For whom:** women 42–62 in peri/menopause, symptomatic, HRT-hesitant, sceptical after a generic supplement that did nothing.
- **Why it wins (straight from Part 1):** it beats Happy Mammoth on **trust**, not spend; it beats Midi/Alloy by being **non-hormonal** (the thing HRT can't claim); it beats the faceless clinical brands (Bonafide) by having **a real human face and warmth.** It fills the empty seat.

## 2.2 Landing page

**Page type — a dedicated long-form landing page, not the catalog PDP.**
Cold paid-social traffic has no brand context and one nervous question: *"will this actually help me, and is it safe?"* The standard Shopify PDP answers neither well. A dedicated page lets us control the narrative, handle objections, and present the offer in one branded scroll. Tradeoff: it costs more to build than a PDP tweak — justified because it is the single highest-leverage surface for cold conversion, and becomes the reusable template for every future angle test.

**Layout (10 sections, each earning its place):**
1. **Hero** — headline + subhead + product shot + primary CTA + trust bar. Sells the promise and offer in one screen.
2. **Problem agitation** — a symptom checklist that mirrors her.
3. **Founder turn (Naomi Watts)** — the credibility pivot competitors can't copy.
4. **How it works / the science** — hormone-free mechanism + clinical backing; de-risks the #1 objection.
5. **What's inside** — ingredient → benefit map.
6. **Social proof** — reviews, ratings, press.
7. **Why Stripes vs. the alternatives** — vs. HRT and vs. generic supplements.
8. **The offer** — subscribe-and-save, guarantee.
9. **FAQ** — safety, timing, cancel-anytime.
10. **Final CTA + risk reversal.**
A sticky Add-to-Cart bar follows the scroll on mobile (80%+ of paid-social traffic).

**Full copy (real, not placeholder):**

- **Hero H1:** "Menopause kept you up. This is how you take the night back."
- **Hero subhead:** "A hormone-free daily from Naomi Watts — clinically-backed support for sleep, mood and mental clarity. Feel the difference or your money back."
- **CTA:** "Start my daily →"  ·  trust bar: "★★★★★ Trusted by thousands · Hormone-free"
- **Problem:** "Does this sound like your last six months? Wide awake at 2 a.m., then wrecked all day. Words vanishing mid-sentence. Mood that swings before you can catch it. And everyone telling you to just push through."
- **Founder (Naomi Watts):** "I went into menopause early, and blindsided. I built Stripes so no woman feels as unprepared as I did. The Inside Addition is the daily I wanted and couldn't find — hormone-free, backed by real science, made to work on the symptoms that actually derail your day."
- **Science:** "Hormone-free, because relief shouldn't mean a trade-off. Clinically-studied botanicals and micronutrients chosen for the peri/menopause symptom cascade — no estrogen, no progestin, one daily dose." *(Assumption: exact ingredients/doses to be locked from the live PDP and reviewed for ad-claim compliance.)*
- **Offer:** "Subscribe & Save 20% — first month ships today, then a fresh supply monthly. Free shipping · Cancel anytime · 60-day feel-it-or-refund guarantee." *(Assumption: offer mechanics are a recommendation; align to actual margin & policy.)*
- **Final CTA:** "Give your body the daily it's been asking for. Try it risk-free for 60 days."

**Visual guidelines:**
- **Imagery:** real women 45–60 in natural light, unretouched, genuinely at ease (rested mornings, not clinical). Product shown in real life, not floating on white. Founder used sparingly, for the credibility beat.
- **Tone:** warm, direct, in-on-it. Confident, never fear-mongering. Permission, not panic.
- **Layout & feel:** editorial whitespace, mobile-first, one CTA color used only for actions, warm creams over sterile white, fast-loading.

## 2.3 Funnel wireframe
Each step is a chance to earn revenue or trust.
1. **Paid-social ad** — angle-led hook (acquire attention).
2. **Landing page** — educate, prove, present the offer (convert cold → buyer).
3. **Cart + checkout** — frictionless, trust badges, express pay (reduce abandonment).
4. **One-click post-purchase upsell** — add a topical or a second bottle (lift AOV, no added CAC).
5. **Thank-you page** — set expectations, invite to community (build trust early).
6. **Onboarding flow (email/SMS)** — how to use, what to expect and when (reduce early churn).
7. **Replenish & retain** — subscription nudges, a day-30 check-in (protect LTV).
8. **Advocate & cross-sell** — reviews/UGC ask, then the wider "scalp to vag" range (compound revenue).

The first order buys the customer. Post-purchase (steps 4–8) is where the model actually makes money.

## 2.4 Measuring success
**North star: LTV : CAC ≥ 3:1, with CAC payback under 3 months.**

- **Acquisition:** ad CTR (≥ ~1.5%), efficient CPC/CPM, cost per landing-page visit trending down.
- **Conversion:** landing-page CVR on cold traffic (target ~3–5%), AOV (lifted by the upsell), subscribe take-rate (target ~30%+), upsell take-rate (~10–15%).
- **Retention / LTV:** month-3 subscription retention (~60%+), 60/90-day repeat rate, refund rate (< ~8%), 90-day LTV vs. CAC.

How they connect: CTR feeds landing-page visits → CVR × AOV sets first-order revenue → subscribe and upsell rates set day-one LTV → retention compounds it → LTV vs. CAC decides whether we scale spend. Targets are launch hypotheses to beat, not promises.

# Part 3 — Funnel Optimization (first A/B tests)

Ranked by leverage over the KPIs. Test 1 is first because Part 1 showed Stripes' current ads are skincare/body and its supplement is unadvertised — so proving a symptom-led supplement page on owned traffic comes before scaling spend.

**Test 1 — PDP/landing hero: symptom-led value prop + proof above the fold.**
- *Hypothesis:* the live product experience opens on the product, not the problem. Leading with the symptom → relief promise + star rating + hormone-free badge above the fold will lift add-to-cart from cold traffic.
- *Change:* rewrite H1 to the symptom→relief promise; surface reviews and the guarantee above the fold; one clear CTA.
- *Where / why first:* top of funnel, highest-traffic surface, and it validates the launch angle before big spend.

**Test 2 — Subscription-first offer presentation.**
- *Hypothesis:* defaulting to Subscribe-and-Save (vs. one-time) with clear savings + cancel-anytime will raise subscription take-rate without hurting overall CVR.
- *Change:* pre-select the subscribe option, restate 20% + free shipping + cancel-anytime, add a value reminder.
- *Where / why first:* the offer step — it moves subscribe take-rate, AOV and LTV directly, and it's cheap to ship.

**Test 3 — Post-purchase one-click upsell.**
- *Hypothesis:* a one-click upsell (topical or 2nd bottle) on the thank-you step lifts AOV with zero added CAC and no checkout-funnel risk.
- *Change:* insert a one-tap upsell between checkout and confirmation.
- *Where / why first:* post-purchase — pure-margin AOV, the safest revenue to add while Tests 1–2 run.

# Part 4 — Agentic Approach & Product

## 4.1 What to automate vs. keep human
- **Agent-led:** competitive research (ad-library scraping, angle mining, traffic monitoring); A/B variant generation, traffic allocation, significance calls and readouts.
- **Agent + human:** creative angle generation (agent proposes, human picks the on-brand, defensible one); landing-page assembly from a template (human edits voice + the founder story); funnel/offer logic (agent wires and flags leaks, human sets economics).
- **Human-led, non-negotiable:** medical / ad-claim compliance; brand voice and founder authenticity.

The rule: agents compress the 80% that's pattern-work; humans keep the 20% that carries brand and legal risk.

## 4.2 The one I'd build — "The Angle-to-Test Loop"
A creative-intelligence and landing-page testing agent. The highest-leverage thing to automate is the loop from *"what's working in the market"* to *"a new landing-page variant live and being tested."*

- **Monitors:** category ad libraries (new creative, angles, ad longevity as a spend proxy); our own ad + landing-page analytics (CTR, CVR, AOV, scroll and drop-off); voice-of-customer (new reviews, support tickets, on-site search).
- **Decides:** which angles are fatiguing (CTR/CVR decay); which fresh angle or page section to test next, ranked by expected lift; when a live test has hit significance.
- **Triggers:** generates a new landing-page variant from a modular template; routes every health claim to a human/regulatory gate before publish; launches the A/B test, reallocates budget to winners, posts a plain-language readout.
- **How:** data pipeline (ad library + analytics + reviews) → an LLM clusters angles and drafts copy against the template → guardrails (brand-voice checks + a hard human gate on claims) → an experimentation API launches and reads the test → a human approves anything customer-facing. The human moves from doing the work to editing and approving it.
