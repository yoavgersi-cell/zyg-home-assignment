# Part 3 - Funnel Optimization

Based on the real stripesbeauty.us pages: the homepage, The Inside Addition PDP, the cart drawer, and checkout. Three tests, in the order I'd run them.

## Test 1 - PDP hero: lead with the symptom, not the spec
**What's there now:** the PDP hero is the product name plus a technical line ("Daily Menopause Symptom Support Probiotic Supplement + Vitamin Blend"). The symptoms it actually helps with - stress, hot flashes, brain fog - are hidden in a collapsed "Details" accordion, and the reviews sit far down (and are mixed with reviews for other products). A cold visitor from a paid ad lands with no emotional hook and no proof up top.
**Hypothesis:** leading with a symptom-led promise + the rating/review count above the fold will lift add-to-cart from cold traffic.
**Change:** swap the technical subhead for a symptom line ("For the hot flashes, brain fog and stress no one warned you about - one hormone-free daily"), pull the top 3 symptoms and the star rating/review count above the fold, and scope the reviews to this product.
**Where:** the PDP (where paid supplement traffic lands and converts).
**Why first:** highest-traffic conversion surface, biggest CVR lever, and it keeps the promise the ad made (message match). Everything downstream depends on winning here.

## Test 2 - Homepage: give the supplement a symptom-led on-ramp
**What's there now:** the homepage is all skincare - the hero is "No Apologies. Just Great Skin." with the Rich & Tight body butter. The Inside Addition appears only near the end of the product grid ($40) and behind a plain "Supplements" tile. A woman who came for symptom relief has to dig to find it.
**Hypothesis:** giving the supplement a symptom-led entry point on the homepage (a dedicated module, or relabeling the "Supplements" tile to a symptom line like "Hot flashes, brain fog & stress?") will route more homepage visitors into the supplement and lift its PDP visits and sales.
**Change:** test a symptom-led supplement module / tile vs. the current plain "Supplements" tile. (Leave the skincare hero alone - Rich & Tight is their bestseller.)
**Where:** the homepage.
**Why second:** the supplement is basically invisible to homepage traffic today; the site leads with skin, and the launch product has no on-ramp for the woman who came for relief.

## Test 3 - Add a one-click post-purchase upsell
**What's there now:** there's a good cart cross-sell ("Spend more, Save more"), but nothing after checkout - the flow goes straight to the Shopify confirmation. That's AOV left on the table.
**Hypothesis:** a one-click upsell on the confirmation step (a topical like Vag of Honor, or a second bottle) lifts AOV with zero added CAC.
**Change:** insert a one-tap post-purchase upsell between payment and confirmation.
**Where:** post-purchase.
**Why third:** pure-margin AOV with no risk to the core funnel - the safest revenue to add, and it can run in parallel with Tests 1-2.

*How they map to the KPIs: Tests 1-2 move conversion rate (and route more traffic to the supplement); Test 3 moves AOV and LTV.*
